# Wave, tsunami, or wake-up call: how to read tonight

## The races that will decide it, when they'll report, and how to tell early what kind of night it is

*The Scope, November 3, 2026*

Tonight everyone will be staring at the same thing: who's ahead, where, and by how much. The trouble is that at 8 p.m., a Democrat "leading" in Florida means nothing until you know *how much they were supposed to lead by*. Election nights only make sense against an expectation. So here's ours, and, more usefully, how to tell in the first few hours whether reality is breaking from it.

## Where we start

Our model (the Snow Owl release, 200,000 simulations) gives Democrats a **96%** chance of winning the [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/), with a median of **242 seats to 193**. They're **81%** favorites for the [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/). In the [governors' races](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/), we give them a **74%** chance of holding at least 26 of 50 governorships, with a median of 27.

One number frames everything else: our forecast already assumes a national House vote of roughly **54% Democratic to 45% Republican**, about a 9-point margin. That's a very big Democratic year built in. So tonight's question isn't "is there a wave?" It's **is the wave bigger, smaller, or the same as the polls said?**

## Four possible nights, and how to recognize each

### 1. The expected night

Democrats win most of the seats where we have them favored and split the true toss-ups. They land around 240 House seats and 53 senators. The tells: [VA-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51002-va-2/) (Elaine Luria vs. Rep. Jen Kiggans, 72% D) flips early, and coin flips like [FL-22](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12022-fl-22/), [FL-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12009-fl-9/) and [ME-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/23002-me-2/) break roughly evenly.

### 2. The blue wave

Democrats take seats where Republicans are still slight favorites, the ones we put at 30–45% for Democrats: [VA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51001-va-1/) (Rob Wittman), [PA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42001-pa-1/) (Brian Fitzpatrick), [FL-13](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12013-fl-13/), [FL-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12007-fl-7/), [MI-4](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/26004-mi-4/), [CO-5](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/08005-co-5/), [TX-23](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48023-tx-23/), [NC-3](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37003-nc-3/). In the Senate, [South Carolina](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/45000-south-carolina/) and [Iowa](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/19000-iowa/) both fall. Democrats clear 250 House seats.

### 3. The tsunami

Democrats win where almost nobody expected them to, in districts we give them only 15–25%: [IN-5](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/18005-in-5/) (Victoria Spartz), [VA-5](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51005-va-5/) (Tom Perriello vs. John McGuire), [FL-27](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12027-fl-27/) (María Elvira Salazar), [NC-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37009-nc-9/) (Richard Hudson), [NC-6](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37006-nc-6/), [WI-8](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/55008-wi-8/), [OH-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/39007-oh-7/), [OH-10](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/39010-oh-10/). In the Senate, [Mississippi](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/28000-mississippi/) (33%) or [Montana](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/30000-montana/) (22%). Among governors, [Florida](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/12000-florida/), [Vermont](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/50000-vermont/) or [New Hampshire](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/33000-new-hampshire/). That's a night past 264 Democratic seats, the top of our 80% range.

### 4. Republicans hold the line

This is the scenario raw numbers hide best, because Democrats will still be gaining plenty of seats. The warning sign is Democrats losing seats they were supposed to hold or pick up comfortably. The House majority runs through the 218th-best seat for Democrats, which for us is [OH-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/39009-oh-9/), Rep. Marcy Kaptur's seat (77% D). Right around it: [PA-10](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42010-pa-10/) (Scott Perry), [MI-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/26007-mi-7/), [IA-3](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/19003-ia-3/), [WI-3](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/55003-wi-3/), [NJ-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/34007-nj-7/), [NY-17](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/36017-ny-17/) (Mike Lawler). If Republicans hold several of those *and* knock off Democrats like [FL-25](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12025-fl-25/) (Jared Moskowitz) or [NC-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37001-nc-1/) (Don Davis), the House is a real race again. The Senate takes much less; more on that below.

## The needle: the best early read of the night

"Leading" counts mislead because every state counts in a different order: early votes first here, big cities last there. Our [needle](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/) gets around that. It doesn't tell you who's winning. It measures **how many points the count is running from our forecast**, on average, with each state counting once. It stays on hold until at least three states have 60% of their reporting units in, so one county can't move it.

What it tells you, in practice:

- **Needle at center**: the expected night. Democrats are roughly hitting their 9-point national margin.
- **Needle 3 points toward Republicans**: the Senate is back in doubt. Kansas, Texas and Alaska, where our forecast has Democrats ahead by 2 to 3 points, would tip Republican, leaving the majority to Maine and Ohio. The House still holds.
- **Needle 6 points toward Republicans**: even the House is in play. Our tipping-point seat, OH-9, is projected at about D+6.
- **Needle 3 points toward Democrats**: the blue wave. Iowa and South Carolina lean Democratic, and Mississippi gets close.
- **Needle 5 or more toward Democrats**: tsunami territory. Districts like WI-8 or FL-11, projected around R+5, come into play.

Those thresholds assume a roughly uniform shift across states, which is never quite true. But they give you the order of magnitude, and that's the thing most missing early in the night.

## Hour by hour (Eastern time)

**6 p.m.: first temperature check.** Eastern Indiana and Kentucky close. Two districts to watch right away: [KY-6](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/21006-ky-6/) around Lexington, a genuine coin flip (47% D), and [IN-5](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/18005-in-5/) north of Indianapolis, a tsunami seat (18% D). A competitive Democrat in IN-5 would be the first sign of something unusual.

**7 p.m.: the Virginia ladder.** Virginia offers an almost perfect ladder, safest to longest shot: [VA-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51007-va-7/) (Eugene Vindman, a hold), [VA-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51002-va-2/) (expected night), [VA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51001-va-1/) (wave), [VA-5](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51005-va-5/) (tsunami). The rung where Democrats stop tells you a lot.

Also at 7: Georgia ([governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/13000-georgia/), 63% D; [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/13000-georgia/), Jon Ossoff at 95%), [South Carolina](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/45000-south-carolina/), New Hampshire, Vermont and most of Florida. Florida has a ladder of its own: [FL-25](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12025-fl-25/) to defend, [FL-22](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12022-fl-22/) and [FL-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12009-fl-9/) at 50-50, [FL-13](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12013-fl-13/) and [FL-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12007-fl-7/) for a wave, [FL-27](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12027-fl-27/) and [FL-11](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12011-fl-11/) for a tsunami. Florida counts fast, so it often gives the first real signal.

**7:30 p.m.: North Carolina and Ohio.** Ohio has the [governorship](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/39000-ohio/) that makes the majority, Sherrod Brown's [Senate race](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/39000-ohio/), and the House tipping-point seat, [OH-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/39009-oh-9/). If Kaptur is struggling, be careful. In North Carolina: [NC-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37001-nc-1/) and [NC-11](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37011-nc-11/) (expected), [NC-3](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37003-nc-3/) (wave), [NC-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37009-nc-9/) and [NC-6](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37006-nc-6/) (tsunami).

**8 p.m.: the big wave of closings.** Nineteen states close. [Texas](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/48000-texas/) (Talarico vs. Paxton), [Maine](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/23000-maine/) and [Kansas](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/20000-kansas/) settle much of the Senate between them. The [Texas governor's race](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/48000-texas/) is the closest governor's race in the country (Greg Abbott, 60% R). Pennsylvania's ladder runs from [PA-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42007-pa-7/) and [PA-8](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42008-pa-8/) to [PA-10](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42010-pa-10/) (tipping point) and [PA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42001-pa-1/) (wave). On the Texas border: [TX-15](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48015-tx-15/), [TX-35](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48035-tx-35/) and [TX-23](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48023-tx-23/). In Michigan, [MI-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/26007-mi-7/) (tipping point) and [MI-4](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/26004-mi-4/) (wave).

**9 p.m.: Iowa, Arizona, Colorado, Wisconsin, New York.** [Iowa](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/19000-iowa/) has the closest Senate race in the country (52% D) and a full House ladder: [IA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/19001-ia-1/), [IA-3](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/19003-ia-3/), [IA-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/19002-ia-2/). In [Nebraska](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/31000-nebraska/), Pete Ricketts faces an independent (28%).

**10 p.m.: Nevada and Montana.** [Nevada](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/32000-nevada/) counts slowly. A Democratic win in [Montana](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/30000-montana/) would be a Senate tsunami signal, and [MT-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/30001-mt-1/) is 51-49.

**11 p.m. and midnight: the West and Alaska.** [CA-40](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/06040-ca-40/), [Oregon](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/41000-oregon/), then [Alaska](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/02000-alaska/) (Peltola, 66%). California and Alaska will take days. If the Senate comes down to Alaska, we won't know tonight.

## Where we sit among the forecasters

We compare our forecast with everyone else's daily on our [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/compare-forecasts/), [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/compare-forecasts/) and [governors](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/compare-forecasts/) comparison pages, and track the markets on our [Prediction markets](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/prediction-markets/) page.

**House: consensus.** Silver Bulletin has Democrats at 95%, The Economist 94%, Split Ticket 92%, Decision Desk HQ 84% (with 241 Democratic seats, right next to our 242), FiftyPlusOne 99%. On the ratings side, Sabato's Crystal Ball, which leaves no toss-ups, puts 239 seats on the Democratic side. Cook Political Report has 222, with 18 toss-ups, and Inside Elections projects a Democratic gain of 20 to 30. Kalshi and Polymarket are at 94%. Our 96% sits squarely in the pack.

**Senate: we're at the high end.** Silver Bulletin is at 80%, Split Ticket 77%, FiftyPlusOne 76%, DDHQ 65%, The Economist 53%. Kalshi is at 75%, Polymarket 77.5%. Cook keeps 7 races as toss-ups. Sabato calls them all, with 19 of 35 races leaning Democratic, which works out to 53 seats, our median. Our 81% comes mostly from three races where we see Democrats as more competitive than the markets: South Carolina (47% vs. about 32%), Mississippi (33% vs. 15%) and Montana (22% vs. nearly zero).

**Governors: we're more cautious.** Kalshi gives Democrats 86% to win most governorships; we're at 74%. The gap is mostly Ohio (67% for us vs. about 83%) and Nevada (62% vs. about 74%), two races from the list above. Sabato's and DDHQ's ratings both work out to 28 Democratic governors, one more than our median.

In the House, the biggest disagreements go the other way: the markets are *more* Democratic than we are in [TX-15](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48015-tx-15/) (89% for Bobby Pulido vs. our 49%), [TX-35](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48035-tx-35/) (75% vs. 47%) and [PA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42001-pa-1/) (63% vs. 36%). Tonight we find out who was right.

**And our own second opinion.** In our [Labs](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/), Orbit, an experimental engine built on different principles, runs alongside Snow Owl. It's more cautious: 86% for the [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/house/), 58% for the [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/senate/) (as of November 2). A 50/50 blend of the two engines, which beat both in Quebec's October 5 election, puts the Senate at 69%. We show both engines, including when they disagree.

## Early voting: a clue, not a result

Our [Early vote](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/early-vote/) page tracks eleven states. Compared with the same point in 2022, the gap between registered Democrats and Republicans among voters has grown in North Carolina (+12.3 vs. +6.9) and Georgia (+9.5 vs. +1.1, an estimate, since Georgia has no party registration). It has narrowed in Kansas, where Republicans now lead by just 2.4 points, down from 11. But in Pennsylvania, Iowa and Arizona, the Democratic edge is *smaller* than in 2022.

Registration isn't a vote, unaffiliated voters are a growing share almost everywhere, and none of this feeds our model, so it doesn't change our probabilities. G. Elliott Morris of FiftyPlusOne made the same point today. Turnout reports show about a 10-point Democratic enthusiasm edge, he wrote, but "what really matters in close races is how the independents break" ([source](https://x.com/gelliottmorris/status/2117706177756721526)). On Florida, he doesn't think the numbers are good enough for a Democratic win, though "it depends on how the NPAs break" ([source](https://x.com/gelliottmorris/status/2117701050966700379)). He's running an [Election Day live blog](https://x.com/gelliottmorris/status/2117705685198610651).

## Why follow the night on Vote-Scope

Plenty of sites will put up numbers without context tonight: a percentage, a tally, a map filling in. The numbers are accurate; they just don't tell you what they mean. A Democrat at 55% with 30% counted can lose if the early votes came in first. A Republican winning a district by 3 can be bad news for the party if the forecast had them winning it by 10. It takes analysis to understand what the numbers show.

- **Fastest Call**, our decision desk. Every race has its own thresholds, set *before* Election Day and applied to the official count. No call goes out without a human sign-off. Each call is time-stamped and shareable in one click, as text, a link or an image. Calls appear on the [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/), [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/) and [governors](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/) pages.
- **The needle**, to know before everyone else whether the night is running bluer or redder than expected, and by how much.
- **Two engines**, Snow Owl and Orbit, side by side.
- **Locked forecasts.** This morning's numbers are frozen. Tonight they meet reality, and our hits and misses stay on the record.

See you at 6 p.m. Eastern: [vote-scope.com/en/us/](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/).
