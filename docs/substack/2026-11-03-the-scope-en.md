# Midterms night: what to watch, hour by hour

## The poll closings that matter, what early voting can and can't tell you, and where Vote-Scope sits among the forecasts

*The Scope, November 3, 2026*

Polls start closing at 6 p.m. Eastern. Before the numbers start scrolling, here's where things stand.

Our model (the Snow Owl release, 200,000 simulations) gives Democrats a **96%** chance of flipping the [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/), with a median outcome of **242 Democratic seats to 193 Republican**. In the [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/), Democrats are **81%** favorites. In the [governors' races](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/), we give them a **74%** chance of holding at least 26 of the 50 governorships, with a median of 27.

Favored is not the same as certain. A one-in-five chance of losing the Senate happens all the time, and the night will come down to about twenty very close races. The full US desk is here: [vote-scope.com/en/us/](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/).

## 1. The night, hour by hour (Eastern time)

**6 p.m.: Indiana and Kentucky.** Only the eastern parts of both states close at 6; the rest follow at 7. The [Kentucky Senate race](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/21000-kentucky/) isn't the story: Republican Andy Barr is a 94% favorite. Watch [KY-6](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/21006-ky-6/) around Lexington instead. It's a true coin flip (47% Democratic), and one of the first competitive districts to report.

**7 p.m.: Georgia, South Carolina, Virginia, New Hampshire, Vermont, and most of Florida.**
- [Georgia governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/13000-georgia/): Keisha Lance Bottoms (D) at 63%. Jon Ossoff is a clear favorite for [Georgia's Senate seat](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/13000-georgia/) (95%).
- [South Carolina Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/45000-south-carolina/): one of the two closest Senate races in the country, 47% for the Democrat. An upset here would say a lot about the night.
- Florida has a cluster of House battlegrounds. [FL-22](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12022-fl-22/) and [FL-9](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12009-fl-9/) are within a tenth of a point of 50-50, and [FL-13](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12013-fl-13/), [FL-7](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12007-fl-7/), [FL-16](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12016-fl-16/) and [FL-25](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/12025-fl-25/) are all in play. Statewide, Republicans remain favored for [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/12000-florida/) (90%) and [governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/12000-florida/) (77%). Florida counts fast, so its margin will be an early read.
- [VA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/51001-va-1/): Republican incumbent Rob Wittman at 59%.
- Two Republican governors holding on: [New Hampshire](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/33000-new-hampshire/) (Kelly Ayotte, 83%) and [Vermont](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/50000-vermont/) (Phil Scott, 80%).

**7:30 p.m.: North Carolina, Ohio, West Virginia.**
- [Ohio Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/39000-ohio/): Sherrod Brown (D) against Sen. Jon Husted, 71% Brown. [Ohio governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/39000-ohio/): Amy Acton (D) at 67% against Vivek Ramaswamy.
- [North Carolina Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/37000-north-carolina/): Roy Cooper (D) at 95%. In the House, [NC-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37001-nc-1/), [NC-11](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/37011-nc-11/) and [OH-15](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/39015-oh-15/) are toss-ups.

**8 p.m.: the big wave.** Nineteen states close, including Pennsylvania, Michigan, Maine, and most of Texas and Kansas.
- [Texas Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/48000-texas/): James Talarico (D) vs. Ken Paxton (R), 67% Talarico. The [Texas governor's race](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/48000-texas/) is the closest governor's race in the country: Greg Abbott (R) at 60%. El Paso closes an hour later.
- Three Texas border districts: [TX-15](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48015-tx-15/) (49% D), [TX-35](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48035-tx-35/) (47% D) and [TX-23](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48023-tx-23/) (38% D).
- [Maine Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/23000-maine/): Susan Collins (R) vs. Troy Jackson (D), 72% Jackson. And [ME-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/23002-me-2/), Matthew Dunlap (D) vs. Paul LePage (R), at 51-49.
- [Kansas Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/20000-kansas/): 70% for the Democrat against Sen. Roger Marshall, while the [Kansas governor's race](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/20000-kansas/) leans to Republican Ty Masterson (60%).
- [Michigan Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/26000-michigan/) (86% D) and [Mississippi Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/28000-mississippi/) (67% for Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith).
- House: [PA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42001-pa-1/) (Brian Fitzpatrick, R, at 64%), [MO-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/29002-mo-2/) (Ann Wagner, R, at 53%), [MI-4](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/26004-mi-4/) and [AL-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/01002-al-2/).

**9 p.m.: Iowa, Arizona, Colorado, Wisconsin, New York, Nebraska.**
- [Iowa Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/19000-iowa/): **the closest Senate race in the country.** Josh Turek (D) vs. Ashley Hinson (R), 52-48. Rob Sand (D) is an 82% favorite for [Iowa governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/19000-iowa/), and [IA-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/19002-ia-2/) is a toss-up.
- [Wisconsin governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/55000-wisconsin/) (78% D) and [WI-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/55001-wi-1/) (Bryan Steil, R, at 53%).
- [AZ-2](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/04002-az-2/) (Jonathan Nez, D, vs. Eli Crane, R, at 51-49), [NY-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/36001-ny-1/), [CO-3](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/08003-co-3/) and [CO-5](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/08005-co-5/).
- [Nebraska Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/31000-nebraska/): incumbent Pete Ricketts faces an independent, whom we give 28%.

**10 p.m.: Nevada and Montana.** [Nevada governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/32000-nevada/): Aaron Ford (D) at 62% against incumbent Joe Lombardo. [MT-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/30001-mt-1/) is 51-49. Nevada counts slowly; don't expect a verdict tonight.

**11 p.m.: the West Coast.** [Oregon governor](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/41000-oregon/) (Tina Kotek, 78%) and [CA-40](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/06040-ca-40/). California will take days, maybe weeks, to finish counting.

**Midnight: Alaska and Hawaii.** [Alaska Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/02000-alaska/): Mary Peltola (D) at 66% against Sen. Dan Sullivan. Probably no result for several days.

The short version: if Democrats win South Carolina or Iowa, they're having a good Senate night. If they lose Texas and Maine, the majority moves west, possibly as far as Alaska, and we may not know it until tomorrow or later.

## 2. Early voting: clues, not results

We track early voting in eleven states on our [Early vote](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/early-vote/) page. Some markers, each measured at the same point before Election Day as in past cycles:

- **North Carolina**: 2.8 million ballots already cast, 131% of the 2022 total at the same stage. Registered Democrats lead registered Republicans among voters by 12.3 points, up from 6.9 in 2022.
- **Georgia**: Democrats +9.5, up from +1.1 in 2022. Georgia has no party registration, so this split is estimated from past primary participation.
- **Kansas**: Republicans lead by only 2.4 points, down from 11 in 2022.
- **Florida**: Republicans still lead, but by 2.4 points instead of 6.5.
- **Pennsylvania, Iowa, Arizona**: the Democratic edge is *smaller* than in 2022. In Pennsylvania, part of that is Republicans who now vote by mail instead of on Election Day.

So the picture is more mixed than "Democrats are ahead." Three caveats:

1. **Registration is not a vote.** A registered Democrat can vote Republican, and vice versa. These numbers tell you who voted, not how.
2. **Unaffiliated voters are a growing share** in nearly every state we track, and nobody knows yet how they voted.
3. **None of this changes our probabilities.** Early vote data doesn't feed our model. We show it for context, not prediction.

G. Elliott Morris of FiftyPlusOne made the same point today. Turnout reports show roughly a 10-point Democratic enthusiasm edge, he wrote, but you shouldn't read too much into it, because "what really matters in close races is how the independents break" ([source](https://x.com/gelliottmorris/status/2117706177756721526)). On Florida specifically, he doesn't think the numbers are good enough for a Democratic win, but says "it depends on how the NPAs break" ([source](https://x.com/gelliottmorris/status/2117701050966700379)). He's also running an [Election Day live blog](https://x.com/gelliottmorris/status/2117705685198610651).

## 3. How Vote-Scope compares with other models and the markets

A forecast on its own tells you little; you need to see it next to the others. We log the public forecasts every day on our comparison pages ([House](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/compare-forecasts/), [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/compare-forecasts/), [governors](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/compare-forecasts/)) and track Kalshi and Polymarket on our [Prediction markets](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/prediction-markets/) page.

**On the House, everyone sees the same thing.** The forecasts we track give Democrats between 83% and 99%; Kalshi is at 94%, Polymarket at 94.5%. Our 96% sits in the pack. FiftyPlusOne, which Morris co-founded, is at 99%.

**On the Senate, we're at the high end.** Our 81% compares with 75% on Kalshi and 77.5% on Polymarket; the tracked models range from 53% to 80%, with FiftyPlusOne at 76%. We're upfront about the gap. Most of it comes from three states where we see Democrats as far more competitive than the markets do: [South Carolina](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/45000-south-carolina/) (47% for us, about 32% in the markets), [Mississippi](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/28000-mississippi/) (33% vs. 15%) and [Montana](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/30000-montana/) (22% vs. almost nothing). In [Texas](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/seats/48000-texas/), it runs the other way: the markets are a bit more bullish on Talarico than we are (74% vs. 67%).

**On governors, we're more cautious than the markets.** Kalshi gives Democrats 86% to win most governorships; we're at 74%. The gap is concentrated in [Ohio](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/39000-ohio/) (67% for us, about 83% in the markets) and [Nevada](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/races/32000-nevada/) (62% vs. about 74%).

In the House, the biggest disagreements run the other direction: in a handful of districts, the markets are *more* Democratic than we are. In [TX-15](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48015-tx-15/), they give Democrat Bobby Pulido 89%; we have him at 49%. In [TX-35](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/48035-tx-35/), it's 75% vs. 47%; in [PA-1](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/districts/42001-pa-1/), Kalshi has the Democrat at 63%, we have 36%. Tonight we find out who was right.

**A second opinion, in-house.** In our Labs, [Orbit](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/), an experimental engine built on different principles, runs alongside the main model. It gives Democrats 86% for the [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/house/) and only 58% for the [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/senate/) (as of November 2). A 50/50 blend of the two engines, which beat either one alone in Quebec's October 5 election, puts the Senate at 69%. [Governors](https://vote-scope.com/en/labs/governors/) are there too. When our two engines disagree, we show it.

## 4. Tonight on Vote-Scope

Plenty of sites will put up raw numbers tonight: a percentage, a "leading" count, a map filling in with color. A number without context can mislead. A Democrat at 55% with 30% of the vote in can still lose if the mail ballots, or the big cities, came in first. It takes analysis to understand what the numbers show. That's what we do.

**Fastest Call, our decision desk.** Every call rests on thresholds computed *before* Election Day, race by race, applied to the official count. No call goes out without a human sign-off. Each call is time-stamped and shareable in one click, as text, a link or an image. Calls appear on the [House](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/house/), [Senate](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/senate/) and [governors](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/governors/) pages.

**The Vote-Scope needle.** It doesn't tell you who's winning. It measures how far the count is running from our forecast, on average, state by state (each state counts once). It stays on hold until at least three states have 60% of their reporting units in, so a single county can't move it. When it tips, the real count is running more Democratic or more Republican than expected in several states at once. That's the best early signal of a wave, or of no wave at all.

**Two engines, side by side.** Snow Owl, our main model, sets the probabilities. Orbit, in the Labs, offers a second read. You see both.

**Locked-in numbers.** This morning's forecasts are frozen. Tonight we hold them up against reality, and our hits and misses stay on the record.

Follow the night with us from 6 p.m. Eastern: [vote-scope.com/en/us/](https://vote-scope.com/en/us/).

Enjoy election night.
